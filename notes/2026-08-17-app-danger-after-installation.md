# The App Can Turn Dangerous After Installation

An APK can look quiet on the day it is installed and behave differently later. That is why Android’s newer security work watches actions as well as files.

Google says Live Threat Detection uses on-device AI to examine app behavior. Android 17 expands the signals to include suspicious SMS forwarding and accessibility overlays that can place hidden content over the screen. Dynamic monitoring can also warn about apps that hide or change their icon, start from the background, or abuse accessibility permissions. Google says these protections will roll out during the second half of 2026.

These are concrete warning signs for a fake messaging app. SMS forwarding can expose verification codes. An accessibility overlay can steer a tap toward a permission or payment the screen did not appear to request. A hidden icon makes the app harder to find after it begins running in the background. None of those behaviors belongs in a simple chat client without a clear, narrow explanation.

A download check should continue after installation. Review the app’s accessibility, SMS, notification, device-admin, and battery-exemption access. Pay attention when the icon disappears or the phone shows a warning about background activity. Remove the app if its permissions expand without a feature that explains them. If the device reports suspicious behavior, keep the warning visible long enough to record the app name and package before uninstalling it.
