# Every Linked Device Is Another Place to Review

[Signal’s August 4 update](https://signal.org/blog/linked-devices-and-android-tablets/) made a second Android phone or tablet a normal linked device. A user can choose to transfer complete message history and the last 45 days of saved media, with the transfer protected by end-to-end encryption. That is a substantial convenience improvement, especially for people who move between a phone, tablet, and laptop.

It also makes device hygiene more visible. A secure synchronization process protects data while it moves. It cannot decide whether an old tablet in a drawer should still receive new conversations.

[X takes a different path](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/about-chat) in its current Chat documentation. It says there is no limit to the number of devices that can use Chat on X. That makes the account’s device list part of the privacy boundary. Each active device is another screen where messages can appear, another local store to protect, and another session to remove when ownership changes.

The sensible habit is boring and effective. Review linked devices after replacing a phone, returning a work computer, lending a tablet, or seeing an unfamiliar login alert. Remove sessions you no longer recognize or use. Then check the platform’s current instructions before assuming that deleting a local app also revokes the remote session.

Signal’s new release is a good reminder that “supports more devices” is only half of a comparison. History-transfer choices, media limits, device removal, and key recovery shape the actual experience.
